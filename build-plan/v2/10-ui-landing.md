# 10 — UI: landing page and judge guide

**Goal:** a stranger understands what Signoff is within ten seconds, and a judge knows exactly what to click within thirty.
**Time:** 4 hours.
**Before you start:** Stages 7 and 8 done, so the landing page links to screens that already look right.

---

## The ten-second test

Show the page to someone who has not seen the project. Ask only: "What does this do?" If they say "an AI that handles PayPal disputes for small shops, but it can only do what the shop allows" — pass. If they say "an AI dashboard" — fail, rewrite the hero.

Do this test twice: once with you present, once by sending the link with no explanation.

---

## Step 1 — Rewrite the hero (45 min)

The current hero reads "AI-Driven Dispute Defense with Hard Signing Limits" with the eyebrow "Autonomous PayPal Dispute Operations". Both are banned by your own anti-slop rules and by `refs/copy-rules.md`. Replace them.

**PROMPT.**

> Rewrite the hero in `signoff/web/src/features/site/LandingPage.tsx`:
>
> 1. **Headline**, at most 12 words, says what it does and who for, in plain words. Use: "PayPal disputes, handled — but only as far as you allow." Do not mention AI in the headline; AI is the how, not the what.
> 2. **Sub**, two sentences maximum: what happens today without it (a deadline, evidence to gather, money lost by default), and what happens instead (an agent prepares and acts, inside limits you set, and asks before anything bigger).
> 3. **Two actions**: **See a dispute handled** (primary, links to `/app/pool`) and **Read the 3-minute guide** (secondary, links to `/guide`).
> 4. Directly under the hero, one line of proof: "Every action below runs against the real PayPal sandbox. Nothing here is a mock." Only use this sentence if Stage 5 is done and true.
> 5. Remove the eyebrow chip, or replace it with the sandbox notice.

**Check:** the ten-second test passes.

## Step 2 — The four blocks (75 min)

**PROMPT.**

> Below the hero, in this order, each one screen-height at most:
>
> 1. **The problem**, with three numbers and their sources. Every number needs a link to a primary source (PayPal's own documentation, not a blog). If you cannot source a number, cut the number, keep the sentence.
> 2. **How it works**, four steps, each with a 12px number, a 15px title and one sentence: PayPal opens a dispute → Signoff gathers the evidence from your store → a fixed set of rules decides what it may do → it acts, or asks you. Use a horizontal layout on desktop, vertical on mobile.
> 3. **The signing limit**, the differentiator. Show a real screenshot of the Charter page next to three short sentences about what an approval can and cannot override: it can raise your own limits, it can never override PayPal's rules, a resolved dispute, or a duplicate action.
> 4. **Connect your store**, with one screenshot of the Connect screen and one sentence: "Signoff reads orders, shipments and messages through one adapter. Shopify is the next implementation." Do not claim Shopify works unless Stage 13 is done.
>
> Every section uses real screenshots from stages 7-9, taken at 1440px, saved under `signoff/web/public/shots/`. No illustrations, no abstract shapes, no stock photography.

**Check:** every screenshot on the page is of the real app, and each one is current.

## Step 3 — The judge guide (`/guide`) (75 min)

This page is what a judge opens second. It must answer "how do I test this" without any prior knowledge.

**PROMPT.**

> Rebuild `/guide` as a numbered five-step path, each step a card with a heading, one sentence, and the exact thing to click:
>
> 1. **Open a demo dispute.** Button linking to `/app/pool`. One sentence: the agent has not run yet, you will watch it.
> 2. **Watch it work.** Explain the four timeline stages in one sentence each. Tell them to wait ~20 seconds.
> 3. **Approve something.** Link to the dispute that needs approval. One sentence on what they are signing.
> 4. **See a prompt injection blocked.** Link to the injection dispute, and one sentence on what to look for: a message asking for a refund, and a recorded decision that refuses it.
> 5. **Ask the dashboard.** Link to `/app`, one sentence on the four questions it can answer.
>
> Then a **What is real** table: disputes, evidence, offers, webhooks — real PayPal sandbox. Orders and tracking — simulated shop. Reasoning — a real model behind a server proxy. Decisions — generated by a deterministic rule set you can read.
>
> Then **Run it yourself** with the one Docker command from the README and the five ports.
>
> Delete the fake sandbox credentials card (`alex.morgan@example.test`). If judges need to log into PayPal, give the real sandbox buyer email and password in this card and nowhere else. If they never need to log in, remove the card entirely.

**Check:** follow the five steps on a phone, in a fresh browser, in under three minutes, without opening a terminal.

## Step 4 — Remove the dead ends (20 min)

**PROMPT.**

> Audit every link on the landing page and the guide:
> 1. Every link resolves to a real route. Run a link check over `/`, `/guide`, `/app`, `/app/pool`, `/app/disputes`, `/app/charter`.
> 2. No link points at `localhost`.
> 3. No placeholder text remains: search for `TODO`, `placeholder`, `coming soon`, `example.test`, `lorem`.
> 4. The footer carries the repo link, the licence, and the video link placeholder.

**Check:** the grep in step 3 returns nothing on the rendered pages.

## Step 5 — Accessibility and performance (30 min)

**PROMPT.**

> On `/` and `/guide`:
> 1. One `<h1>`; headings descend without skipping.
> 2. Every image has meaningful `alt` text describing what the screenshot shows.
> 3. Colour contrast meets AA for all text.
> 4. The page works with JavaScript disabled enough to read the hero and the problem section (no blank page).
> 5. Largest contentful paint under 2.5s on a throttled connection; images are served as compressed WebP.

## If it goes wrong

**The page still feels generic.** You are describing the product instead of the merchant's day. Replace one paragraph with a specific moment: "It is Friday, a $38 dispute has two days left, and the tracking number that would win it is in an email from June."

**The screenshots look different from the live app.** You changed the app after taking them. Retake them on the same day as the video.

---

## Cut

In this order: the numbers block (replace with one sourced sentence), the store-connection section (move it into the guide), the second hero action.
